Layer 1 Overview · Human-Centric Authoring · 0.0.41

Author a shared tool
with the people, reasons, and boundaries intact.

Authoring a Semantic Instrument starts with a human purpose — a clinical checklist, a community referral rule, a professional rubric, or a multi-party agreement — and helps contributors make vocabulary, evidence, authority, privacy, and review explicit before software executes a single step.

This guide moves from a plain-language overview of the authoring workflow to the Four-Plane Identifier Fabric (IDF-01–IDF-05), developer morphisms, and formal SHACL and compiler firewall specifications.

Authoring path

From “what are we trying to do?” to a bounded, reviewable instrument

1. Purpose

Name the practical question, affected people, intended benefit, and excluded decisions.

2. Participants

Capture positions, roles, mandates, consent conditions, dissent, and review rights.

3. Meaning & evidence

Link shared terms to sources, claims, assumptions, dates, confidence, and provenance.

4. Boundaries

Set permitted uses, disclosure paths, holds, escalation, privacy, and ZK predicates.

5. Rehearse

Run plain-language examples and edge cases before anyone relies on the rule.

6. Govern release

Ratify, version, publish or keep in Sanctuary custody, then retain run receipts.

The workspace people need

Complementary views of one accountable instrument

Plain-language brief

Purpose, affected people, benefit, risks, excluded outcomes, and the narrow question the tool is authorised to answer. The executable rule stays traceable to this readable brief.

Agreement and authority

A place for people to contribute, qualify, dissent, authorise, delegate narrowly, challenge, or withdraw. Legal entities and AI tools always preserve traceable human principal accountability.

Meaning map

A visual, inspectable connection between terms, ontologies, claims, sources, and rules. Domain experts can start visually while ontology engineers work directly with RDF, N3, and SHACL.

Evidence timeline

Shows what was known at decision time ($T_0$), what changed later ($T_1$), which assumptions expired, and which material changes trigger review or remedy.

Disclosure composer

Specifies what a routine verifier learns, what remains in Sanctuary custody, who may request controlled disclosure, under which procedure, and with what auditable receipt.

Rehearsal and review

Tests examples, counter-examples, missing inputs, conflicts, and temporal transitions before activation, showing clearly why a run is ready, held, or escalated.

Layer 2 · Architecture · Identifier Fabric (IDF-01–IDF-05)

Four Disjoint Planes Keep People, Claims, Channels, and Tools Distinct

A common failure in software systems is collapsing a person into an email address, a cryptographic key, a wallet, or a probabilistic score. QualiaDB enforces a strict Four-Plane Identifier Fabric (ontologies/identifier_fabric_shapes.ttl) so authoring tools model human agency, evidentiary claims, network locators, and cryptographic instruments on disjoint planes connected only by typed evidence morphisms.

Plane 1 · Entity (Who Plane) living-SHACL

NaturalAgent · LegalEntity · SystemAgent

Represents accountable actors on the Who plane. idf:NaturalAgent is framed as "living-SHACL" and is structurally disjoint from owl:Thing, idf:Instrument, and idf:SpatiotemporalHandle.

Invariant: Never merged by similarity, handle, wallet, location, or biometrics. owl:sameAs is forbidden on the Who plane (SameAsError::WhoPlaneForbidden).

Plane 2 · Claim Plane Scoped & Signed

VerifiableCredential · ClaimOpinion · RoleCapacityGrant

Holds scoped, time-bounded assertions (idf:AssertionClaim, idf:AccountabilityArtifact, idf:RelianceRecord, idf:AssumptionChain, deontic and epistemic norms) with explicit issuer provenance (idf:assertedBy) and subject reference (idf:aboutSubject).

Invariant: Verifiable Credentials prove cryptographic origin integrity of an issuer's statement, not universal ontological truth.

Plane 3 · Handle Plane Revocable Locator

SpatiotemporalHandle · DNI · did:qi Endpoints

Covers rotatable, relationship-scoped network and directory locators (idf:DigitalNetworkIdentifier, idf:RelationScopedLocator, idf:EnvironmentPredicate, DNS, email, and did:qi endpoints) anchored by idf:frameId and idf:epochTick.

Invariant: Revoking or rotating a handle closes the communication channel without erasing or invalidating the underlying Entity.

Plane 4 · Instrument Plane ProofClass + KeyRole

Instrument · AiAgent · MachineDevice · Keys

Cryptographic keys, hardware tokens, wallets, cancelable biometric templates, and software/AI tools classified by InstrumentProofClass (CryptographicOriginIntegrity, HardwareChannelPossession, SemanticClaimEvaluation) and closed KeyRole (IdentityAssertion, CredentialSigning, TransportHandshake, StorageEncryption, RecoveryBootstrap).

Invariant: Every idf:AiAgent resides on the Instrument plane and requires an explicit idf:sponsorPrincipal.

Layer 3 · Typed Cross-Plane Evidence Morphisms

Explicit Links Across Planes

Rather than equating a person with a key or a network handle, statements traverse planes only through typed evidence predicates: 

@prefix idf: <urn:qualia:idf:> .

# Claim Plane → Instrument Plane (cryptographic origin proof)
ex:claim-042 a idf:ClaimOpinion ;
    idf:plane "Claim" ;
    idf:assertedBy ex:issuer-college ;
    idf:aboutSubject ex:clinician-entity ;
    idf:assertedAbout ex:clinician-entity ;
    idf:supportedByInstrument ex:ml-dsa-65-signing-key ;
    idf:observedAtHandle ex:dni-clinic-session-09 .

# Err(SameAsError::WhoPlaneForbidden)

Layer 4 · Formal SHACL & F5 / F6 Anti-Feature Firewalls

Compile-Time & Runtime Entity Protection

Both the F5 VibeScript compile-time verifier and the F6 runtime ingress/query firewall reject surveillance-scoring and entity-collapsing predicates (person_score, identity_confidence, same_person): 

# From ontologies/identifier_fabric_shapes.ttl (Packet IDF-01)
idf:NaturalAgentShape a sh:NodeShape ;
    sh:targetClass idf:NaturalAgent ;
    sh:property idf:NaturalAgent-planeProp ;    # sh:hasValue "NaturalAgent"
    sh:property idf:NaturalAgent-framingProp ;  # sh:hasValue "living-SHACL"
    sh:not idf:NaturalAgent-notOwlThing ;       # disjoint from owl:Thing
    sh:not idf:NaturalAgent-notInstrument ;     # disjoint from idf:Instrument
    sh:not idf:NaturalAgent-notHandle .         # disjoint from SpatiotemporalHandle

Interaction principles

What makes it human-centric in use

  • Meaning before syntax: people begin in ordinary language, diagrams, examples, and references; formal RDF/N3/SHACL representations remain inspectable at every step.
  • Preserve epistemic status: a claim, assumption, source, dissent, or open question stays distinct from an attested fact.
  • Progressive precision: start with a clear, bounded tool; add constraints, modalities, proofs, and automation only as the purpose requires.
  • Hold visibly on missing preconditions: absent authority, evidence, privacy conditions, or inputs produce an explicit hold or human escalation.
  • Every automation is challengeable: participants can inspect the basis, identify accountable human principals, request review, and issue a new version without overwriting history.

Accountability boundary

The workspace helps people articulate and run rules; authority remains human.

A clear visual interface makes complex rules easier to inspect. To ensure visual clarity never substitutes for due process, the workspace keeps scope, consequences, accountable human authority (idf:sponsorPrincipal), consent, uncertainty, and review pathways visible alongside the model.

Legal entities (LegalEntity) and software tools (idf:AiAgent) always link back to the natural persons acting in authorised roles.

Delivery architecture

The POET Authoring Workspace, Hosted by the Desktop Shell and Grounded in QualiaDB

The authoring experience combines three focused layers: the native Desktop host that manages local hardware and Sanctuary custody boundaries, the POET visual relational workspace where collaborators map concepts and agreements, and the QualiaDB engine that validates SHACL shapes, evaluates modal logic, and issues receipts.

LOCAL DESKTOP HOST

Hardware & custody boundary

Launches POET, manages local .q42 v4 volumes, key vaults, renderer capabilities, and model runtimes, and enforces FLAG_SANCTUARY publication locks.

POET WORKSPACE

Relational authoring canvas

Presents a visual manifold where concepts, sources, claims, rules, party positions, and consequences connect through typed semantic and modal relations.

QUALIADB / WEBIZEN ENGINE

Bounded semantic substrate

Compiles SHACL and N3 rules, enforces the Four-Plane Identifier Fabric and F5/F6 firewalls, evaluates 21 logic modalities, and generates verifiable receipts.

How a selected generative agent participates

An author may invite a local or permitted generative agent (idf:AiAgent on the Instrument plane) to suggest terms, draft plain-language summaries, propose ontology alignments (cml:Proposed), or generate edge-case tests inside a bounded drafting lane.

Declared inputs

The author selects which ontologies, records, references, and tool permissions the agent may access.

Inspectable proposals

Every proposed node, connector, rule, or mapping arrives as an attributed draft that people can accept, amend, or reject.

Human ratification

Only accountable human principals can ratify an agreement, attest a concept (cml:Attested), authorise disclosure, or activate an instrument.

WASM POET · Collaborative Manifolds

Shared browser manifolds with explicit membership and custody boundaries

POET's WebAssembly build lets collaborators inspect and co-author a selected semantic manifold directly in the browser before exporting or activating the package in a local Desktop environment.

Collaborative manifold

Participants inspect the same scoped graph and propose nodes, relations, comments, alternatives, and tests. Competing interpretations remain attributable and versioned until resolved.

Structured connectors

Connectors carry formal semantics—“is defined by”, “supports”, “defeats”, “is permitted by”, or cross-plane evidence morphisms (idf:supportedByInstrument, idf:assertedAbout).

Bounded browser scope

Browser sessions operate on declared ontology packs and sample or permitted records, while custodial keys and Sanctuary volumes remain in the authorised local host.
