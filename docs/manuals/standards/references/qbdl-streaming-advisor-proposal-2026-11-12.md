## 1. Architectural Mandate and Dual-Plane Ecosystem Position

Streaming for the Qualia Bundle format (`QBDL`, carried in `.hmc` Hypermedia Containers) must operate as a native, post-quantum capability of the Qualia Decentralized Network Fabric (QDNF) coupled directly to the [Etalon](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://mediaprophet.github.io/etalon/) hardware, model, and sensing execution engine. Native operation must never depend on HTTP, the Domain Name System (DNS), centralized content delivery networks (CDNs), Web Public Key Infrastructure (Web PKI), blockchains, or external payment processors. HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2, and HTTP/3 ([RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9110/), [RFC 9114](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9114/)) serve strictly as backward-compatible delivery bindings managed across explicit trust boundaries. Cryptographic confidentiality, integrity, and authority binding must conform end-to-end to the Qualia Peer Runtime (QPR) post-quantum replacement security profile (`qpr-pq-1`), anchored in [NIST FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) (ML-KEM), [NIST FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final) (ML-DSA), and [NIST FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) (SLH-DSA). Quantum Processing Unit (QPU) problem generalization and historical solution reuse constitute a first-class workload of the Permissive Commons rather than the sole organizing abstraction of the transport protocol.

A comprehensive architectural design must reflect the actual two-repository structure of the platform. The ecosystem divides responsibilities cleanly across two cooperating planes:

1. **The Semantic, Governance, Network, and Storage Plane (`qualiadb`)**: Owns `QBDL` container framing (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/bundle/`), `.q42` 48-byte `NQuin` semantic volumes (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/q42/`), `.10d` spatial/ontogenetic digital twin containers, `.emf` electromagnetic bricks, `.m64`/`.e64` geodetic matter containers, QDNF routing and `QSession` encrypted transport (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/net/qdnf/`), QPR deterministic kernel scheduling (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/net/peer/`), Webizen ontological contracts, Permissive Commons publication gates, and QPU QUBO compilation (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/qubo_compiler.rs`).
2. **The Silicon, Model, Residency, and Sensing Execution Plane (`etalon`)**: Derived from a hardened fork of `wgpu` `30.0.0` (`e084b66aab767940ff1236604d194c9e1c2844b0`) and `naga`, [Etalon](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://github.com/mediaprophet/etalon) owns cross-platform GPU/accelerator execution across Vulkan, DirectX 12, Metal, CUDA, and browser [W3C WebGPU](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/) (`etalon`, `etalon-core`, `etalon-hal`, `etalon-core-remote`), native `.p64` version 4 weight preparation and companion operator packages (`crates/etalon-model/`), writer-excluding OS byte leases and verified snapshots (`crates/etalon-model-io/`), zero-heap CPU/VRAM residency governors (`crates/etalon-resources/`), streamed Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and Per-Layer Embedding (PLE) inference (`crates/etalon-inference/`), governed artifact acceptance (`crates/etalon-inference-contract/`), 48-byte `NQuin` GPU kernels (`crates/etalon-nquin/`), prepared zero-allocation Vulkan compute (`Adapter::prepare_compute`), and caller-buffered physical sensing (`crates/etalon-sense*`, bridged into QualiaDB via `crates/qualia-etalon-sensing-bridge/`).

Traditional media protocols such as HTTP Live Streaming ([RFC 8216](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8216/)) and MPEG-DASH ([DASH-IF Timing Model](https://dashif.org/Guidelines-TimingModel/)) optimize linear playout along a single presentation clock. By contrast, a `QBDL` bundle encapsulates heterogeneous scientific, cognitive, spatial, and legal artifacts whose partial retrieval obeys directed acyclic dependency graphs (DAGs), hardware page-alignment invariants, VRAM residency ceilings, and ontological rights constraints. Fetching an entity ontology and octahedral impostor atlas (`.10d` `EONT1` and `IMPO1`) enables immediate spatial interaction in under 50 KiB; streaming selected expert tiles or repacked `Q4_K` scale and bit planes (`etalon_model::operator_package`) activates sparse neural inference without loading multi-gigabyte trunk weights; retrieving a 3D Hilbert-indexed electromagnetic brick (`.emf`) or a canonicalized QUBO solution witness satisfies bounded scientific queries without transferring restricted sibling entries.

### 1.1 Domain Terminology and State Machine Separation

Precise specification requires strict separation between container syntax, cryptographic proof, hardware residency, semantic validity, and economic finality:

| Term | Normative architectural meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bundle** | An immutable `QBDL` byte sequence starting with magic `0x5142444C` (`"QBDL"`), containing aligned intact payload entries and a trailing [CBOR (RFC 8949)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8949/) index footer. |
| **Entry** | A standalone, bit-identical embedded file (`BundleEntry`) addressed by logical `key`, format `kind`, aligned absolute `offset`, exact byte `length`, and content digest. |
| **Chunk group (Leaf quantum)** | The fixed power-of-two byte interval (16 KiB or 64 KiB) forming a single leaf in the outer SHA-384 Merkle range-proof tree and random-access AEAD framing. |
| **Segment** | A semantically meaningful, independently verifiable byte interval (one or more contiguous chunk groups, or an entire small entry) declared in the stream descriptor. |
| **Representation** | An explicitly typed alternative encoding of an asset (such as `LOD2` vs. `LOD0` geometry, or `Q4_K` vs. `BF16` weights) governed by a declared mathematical or perceptual compatibility relation. |
| **Generation (Snapshot)** | An immutable publication cut binding a bundle commitment, policy epoch, ontology closure digest, and predecessor lineage. |
| **Residency lease** | A non-cloneable host/device reservation (`etalon_model_io::ModelLease`, `etalon_resources::Reservation`, or QPR `BufferLease`) pinning verified bytes in RAM, NVMe, OPFS, or GPU VRAM. |

Protocol implementations must maintain eight distinct, non-conflated lifecycle states for every streamed unit: (1) **Reserved** (memory, I/O, VRAM, and economic hold admitted), (2) **Transferred** (raw wire bytes arrived), (3) **Cryptographically Verified** (PQ signature chain and SHA-384 Merkle/AEAD tag accepted), (4) **Plaintext Released** (decrypted into an exclusive caller lease after AEAD authentication), (5) **Structurally Admitted** (embedded `.q42`/`.10d`/`.p64`/`.emf`/`.m64` parser invariants satisfied), (6) **Policy & Semantically Accepted** (Webizen SHACL/N3/consent/publication rules satisfied), (7) **Committed to Execution** (`etalon` GPU upload, `PendingDense::commit`, or `AcceptedArtifact` consumption), and (8) **Economically Reconciled** (idempotent receipt settled or discharged against finite obligations). Advancing one state never implies completion of a later state.

## 2. Critical Review of the Initial Brief and Inspected Codebase Audit

### 2.1 Evaluation of the Initial Advisory Draft

Review of the 12 November 2026 initial draft (`qbdl-streaming-expert-advisor-brief.md`) confirms a sound foundational posture on governance and security hygiene. Specifically, the initial draft rightly rejected silent fallback from post-quantum to classical cryptography, identified that 16-byte and 12-byte truncated hash prefixes in legacy container directories are defects rather than valid Bao proofs, separated `QSession` packet loss recovery from application-level semantic prioritization, and preserved the Permissive Commons distinction between operating carriage costs and finite creation-cost recovery.

However, cross-examination against the complete `qualiadb` and `etalon` source trees and current external cryptographic and systems literature reveals six major gaps that prevent the initial draft from serving as a sufficient basis for specification freeze:

1. **Omission of the Etalon Execution, Model, Residency, and Sensing Plane**: The initial brief treated render and inference consumers as abstract downstream callers and did not examine `etalon`. In reality, `etalon` defines concrete binary layouts (`P64` v4 with 16 KiB default pages and the 736-byte `ETLIMD1\0` metadata extension), companion streaming operator packages (`q4k_repack` bit-plane/scale-plane splitting and `SpecialistDelta` anchors), zero-heap VRAM/CPU admission (`ResidencyGovernor`), streamed MoE/PLE execution (`Qwen4ExpStoragePlan`, `execute_streamed_swiglu_with_tiles`), tentative inference transactions (`PendingDense`), governed output contracts (`GovernedArtifactSession`), and hardware sensing sidecars (`qualia-etalon-sensing-bridge`). Ignoring `etalon` leaves page alignment, zero-copy mmap, GPU upload backpressure, and WebGPU browser limits underspecified.
2. **Unresolved Cryptographic Trade-Offs (Advisor Questions A02–A05)**: The initial brief posed open choices between flat digest lists and Merkle trees, left COSE envelope structures undecided, and asked whether commitments should cover plaintext, ciphertext, or both—without enumerating the mathematical overhead or random-access AEAD security model ([Hoang et al. OAE2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/189)) needed to decide them.
3. **Undiagnosed P64 Alignment Incompatibility**: The initial brief noted `QBDL`'s 64-byte default and optional 4096-byte outer alignment flags, but did not catch that `etalon-model` sets `P64_DEFAULT_PAGE_LOG2 = 14` (**16,384 bytes**) and supports `page_log2` up to `30` (`crates/etalon-model/src/p64/layout.rs`). Consequently, even 4096-byte outer bundle alignment breaks zero-copy page alignment for default `.p64` tensors sliced inside a `QBDL` bundle.
4. **Hardcoded Micro-Ceilings in Existing QDNF Replication Primitives**: Inspected QDNF replication modules (`crates/qualia-core-db/src/net/peer/replication/manifest.rs` and `transfer.rs`) hardcode `MAX_RANGES = 8`, `MAX_DECODED_BYTES = 4 MiB`, `MAX_BLOCKS = 8`, and `MAX_BLOCK_BYTES = 16 KiB`. Those constants suffice for small control artifacts and single 40,960-byte `.q42` SuperBlocks (`SUPERBLOCK_CHUNKS_MAX = 3`), but cannot stream multi-gigabyte `.p64` models or `.emf`/`.m64` field volumes without a paged segment-tree extension.
5. **Unformulated Distributed Cap Conservation (Advisor Question A10)**: The initial brief correctly observed that two mirrors cannot independently reserve 7 units when only 7 remain, yet stopped short of specifying the distributed protocol that enforces $S + W + H \le T$ without putting consensus round-trips into packet loops.
6. **Underspecified Canonical QUBO Equivalence (Advisor Question A11)**: The initial brief warned that stripping variable names is insufficient for QPU reuse, but did not specify how floating-point QUBO matrices are normalized, converted under Ising gauge symmetries, canonically labeled, and verified against poisoned remote submissions.

### 2.3 Eight Concrete Codebase Defects Requiring Release Gates

Before any wire schema is frozen, eight specific implementation hazards verified in the codebase must be closed by normative requirements:

1. **Divergent Header Flag and Index Preflight Contracts**: `BundleWriter::with_page_align_4096(true)` sets `BUNDLE_FLAG_PAGE_ALIGN_4096` (`0x0001`), yet `reader_limits::preflight` rejects any bundle where `bytes[6..8] != [0, 0]`. Meanwhile, `HmcProgressiveRangePlanner::from_header_and_index` bypasses `reader_limits::preflight` altogether and calls `ciborium::from_reader`, forfeiting zero-heap preflight bounds on duplicate keys, overlapping entry intervals, indefinite-length CBOR items, nested `meta` depth, and trailing bytes.
2. **P64 16 KiB Page Alignment vs. QBDL 64B/4096B Alignment**: `BundleWriter` pads entries to 64 bytes or 4096 bytes, whereas `etalon_model::p64::layout` defaults to `P64_DEFAULT_PAGE_SIZE = 16,384` bytes (`page_log2 = 14`) and validates `is_page_aligned(offset, page_size)` relative to the start of the `.p64` slice. If a consumer `mmap`s a `QBDL` bundle on a 64-byte or 4096-byte boundary that is not `0 mod 16384`, physical OS/GPU page alignment (`etalon-agent::import_host_memory` and `Q6_K` / SIMD / DMA alignment) is silently lost unless the bundle enforces `max(entry_required_align)` across embedded entries.
3. **Silent Digest Erasure and Length-Only Verification**: In `streaming_loader.rs`, `let expected_sha256 = entry.sha256.as_slice().try_into().ok();` converts a malformed (non-32-byte) digest in the CBOR index into `None` without error. When `verify_arrived_range` subsequently inspects an `HmcRangeRequest` where both `expected_sha256` and `expected_crc32c` are `None`, it returns `Ok(())` solely because `arrived_bytes.len() as u64 == req.length`. Under an adversarial network model, unauthenticated length matching is an immediate remote payload-injection vulnerability.
4. **Unauthenticated CRC-32C Sub-Entry Reliance**: `plan_10d_interior_section_ranges` verifies interior `.10d` sections using only the 32-bit Castagnoli `crc32c` read from the `.10d` section table (`SectionDescriptor.crc32c`). Because CRC-32C is linear over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$, an adversary or poisoned mirror can trivially craft chosen-collision geometry or shader payloads matching any 32-bit CRC unless the `.10d` header/table AND every interior transfer quantum are covered by cryptographic SHA-384 range proofs.
5. **Truncated 16-Byte and 12-Byte Hash Prefixes in Container Directories**: `EmfChunkDirectoryEntry::bao_hash_prefix` (`[u8; 16]`, 128 bits) and `M64ChunkDirectoryEntry::bao_root_hash_prefix` (`[u8; 12]`, 96 bits) provide at most 64-bit and 48-bit birthday collision resistance respectively ($2^{48}$ classical operations, and $2^{32}$ quantum query complexity under Grover/Brassard-Høyer-Tapp collision bounds), and carry neither Merkle interior sibling nodes nor length bindings. They must never be treated as cryptographic chunk authenticators.
6. **Unverified `CborLdManifest` Proofs and Ad Hoc Serialization**: `CborLdManifest` serializes a Rust struct directly with `ciborium` rather than canonical [W3C CBOR-LD 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/cbor-ld-10/) term-table compression, and its loader paths inspect `geometry_layout` and `commons_obligation` without verifying `proof.proof_value` against an authorized controller DID or pinned ontology closure.
7. **Forgeable Local Parity Tokens**: `HydrationCapabilityToken` uses a deterministic XOR/mix parity seal suitable only for catching accidental in-process struct corruption. Passing or accepting such tokens across worker, IPC, or network boundaries without binding them to QPR's unforgeable slot-and-generation `ReservationHandle` violates capability confinement.
8. **Classical `did:qi` Genesis Bottleneck**: `docs/manuals/standards/did-qi-method.md` v0.1.0 derives `did:qi:<id>` from a SHA-256 genesis payload signed solely with classical Ed25519 (`Qi Document Proof v1`). Claiming end-to-end post-quantum authority under `qpr-pq-1` requires a specified `did:qi` PQ controller proof extension (`DualProof` with `ML-DSA-65` + `Ed25519` and SHA-384 update commitments) so an adversary with a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) cannot forge controller updates for an existing `did:qi` identifier.

## 3. State-of-the-Art External Research and Comparative Trade-Off Analysis

Enumerating the optimal streaming architecture requires grounding each protocol layer in established cryptographic, transport, storage, and distributed-systems research rather than bespoke constructions.

### 3.1 Post-Quantum Key Establishment, Dual Signatures, and Verifiable Envelopes

Post-quantum migration across Internet standards has converged on hybrid key establishment and explicit, non-polymorphic signature envelopes:

- **Hybrid KEM Combiners**: [NIST FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) standardizes ML-KEM (`ML-KEM-768` providing NIST Security Category 3), while [NIST SP 800-227](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-227.pdf) (September 2025) and the IRTF CFRG specification [`draft-irtf-cfrg-hybrid-kems`](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-irtf-cfrg-hybrid-kems/) formalize IND-CCA2 hybrid KEM combiners. In TLS 1.3, [RFC 10024](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc10024/) (`X25519MLKEM768`, August 2026) concatenates the 32-byte ML-KEM shared secret followed by the 32-byte X25519 shared secret into [HKDF (RFC 5869)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5869/) while binding both public shares and ciphertexts in the handshake transcript to prevent KEM-recombine attacks. SP 800-227 explicitly emphasizes domain-separated delimiters and transcript binding so that the hybrid remains IND-CCA2 secure if *either* ML-KEM-768 or X25519 remains secure. QPR's `pq_handshake.rs` already adopts the `ml_kem_ss || x25519_ss` ordering; freezing length-prefixed share-transcript inputs completes SP 800-227 compliance for `QSession` and `QLink`, and provides the exact recipient-wrap KEM needed for store-and-forward object encryption.
- **Dual Signatures and COSE Envelopes**: [NIST FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final) standardizes `ML-DSA-65` (public key 1,952 bytes; signature 3,309 bytes), and [NIST FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) standardizes stateless hash-based `SLH-DSA` (`SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s`: public key 64 bytes; signature 29,792 bytes) as a non-lattice root/recovery hedge. For CBOR-native protocols, [RFC 9052](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9052/) defines `COSE_Sign1` and `COSE_Sign`, while [RFC 9964](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9964/) (May 2026) standardizes fully specified COSE algorithm identifiers for ML-DSA (`ML-DSA-44` = `-48`, `ML-DSA-65` = `-49`, `ML-DSA-87` = `-50` with Asymmetric Key Pair `kty = 7` and empty FIPS 204 `ctx` at the primitive layer because context binding occurs in COSE's `Sig_structure`). Furthermore, [RFC 9995](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9995/) (July 2026) standardizes COSE Hash Envelopes for detached payload commitments, and [RFC 9942](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9942/) (June 2026) standardizes COSE Receipts (header parameter `394`) for Verifiable Data Structure (VDS) inclusion and consistency proofs. Adopting RFC 9052 + RFC 9964 + RFC 9942 + RFC 9995 directly resolves QPR's open `CRY-02.05` (`cose_sign1_frozen() == false`) gate using interoperable IETF wire structures rather than custom signature framing.

### 3.2 Authenticated Partial Integrity: Digest Lists vs. Bao vs. SHA-384 Chunk-Group Merkle Trees

To verify arbitrary byte ranges before exposing them to parsers or GPUs, three candidate constructions exist in the literature and production P2P systems:

| Construction | Reference & hash primitive | Metadata size for 16 GiB `.p64` bundle | Proof overhead per random 64 KiB read | Assessment for `qpr-pq-1` QBDL streaming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat segment digest array** | Classic BitTorrent / OCI manifest (`SHA-384` per segment) | 12 MiB at 64 KiB segments (262,144 $\times$ 48 B); 768 KiB at 1 MiB segments | 0 B after descriptor download (12 MiB upfront descriptor penalty!) | Excellent for small bundles ($\le 32$ segments, $\le 1.5\text{ KiB}$ array), prohibitive upfront descriptor latency and cold RAM cost for multi-gigabyte P64/EMF assets. |
| **Stock Bao (1 KiB / 4 KiB leaves)** | [Bao specification](https://github.com/oconnor663/bao) (`BLAKE3` 32 B nodes) | 1 GiB outboard at 1 KiB leaves ($\approx 6.25\%$); 256 MiB at 4 KiB leaves | $\approx 768\text{ B}$ (24 sibling hashes $\times$ 32 B) | Rejected as primary PQ commitment: hardcoded to 256-bit `BLAKE3` (violating `qpr-pq-1` 384-bit SHA-384 collision/commitment policy), un-audited beta format, excessive 6.25% outboard size. |
| **Chunk-group Bao (`abao` / `iroh-blobs`)** | [Iroh BLAKE3 Hazmat & Chunk Groups](https://www.iroh.computer/blog/blake3-hazmat-api), [abao](https://github.com/n0-computer/abao) (16 KiB leaf groups) | 64 MiB outboard ($\approx 0.39\%$); SIMD-friendly 16-chunk subtrees | $\approx 640\text{ B}$ (20 sibling hashes $\times$ 32 B) | Demonstrates the decisive systems lesson: grouping leaves to 16 KiB–64 KiB cuts tree storage by $16\times$–$64\times$ while preserving low-latency verified streaming, though still bound to `BLAKE3`. |
| **Domain-separated SHA-384 Chunk-Group Merkle Tree** | [RFC 9162 §2.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) (`0x00` leaf / `0x01` internal node, `SHA-384` 48 B nodes, 64 KiB or 16 KiB leaf quantum) | 24 MiB total outboard at 64 KiB leaves ($0.14\%$); **only 48 B root** in descriptor! | $\approx 864\text{ B}$ ($18 \times 48\text{ B}$, $1.35\%$ wire overhead for one isolated 64 KiB read; amortized $<0.1\%$ for contiguous reads) | **Selected optimal construction** (paired with inline array shortcut for $\le 32$ segments): satisfies `qpr-pq-1` SHA-384 requirement, keeps the signed stream descriptor tiny ($< 4\text{ KiB}$), supports [RFC 9162 §2.1.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) append consistency proofs, and aligns exactly with 16 KiB P64 pages and 32 KiB EMF bricks. |

The mathematical behavior of [RFC 9162 §2.1.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) over `SHA-384` eliminates ambiguity for non-power-of-two bundle lengths without dummy leaf padding: for $n$ chunk groups $D_n = \{d_0, \dots, d_{n-1}\}$, a single leaf hashes as $\mathrm{MTH}(\{d_0\}) = \mathrm{SHA384}(\mathtt{0x00} \parallel \mathrm{LeafFrame}(d_0))$, and for $n > 1$ with $k$ the largest power of two strictly less than $n$ ($k < n \le 2k$), internal nodes hash as $\mathrm{MTH}(D_n) = \mathrm{SHA384}(\mathtt{0x01} \parallel \mathrm{MTH}(D_{0..k}) \parallel \mathrm{MTH}(D_{k..n}))$. Including the immutable bundle ID, leaf index $m$, and exact byte length $\ell_m$ inside $\mathrm{LeafFrame}(d_m)$ prevents leaf reordering, cross-bundle splicing, and final-chunk truncation while keeping verification stack memory strictly bounded to $\lceil \log_2(n) \rceil \times 48$ bytes ($\le 1,920\text{ B}$ for exabyte-scale objects).

### 3.3 Random-Access Authenticated Encryption for Untrusted Caches and Mirrors

When private or gated Commons bundles traverse untrusted custodial caches, delay-tolerant relays, or HTTP gateways, session encryption (`QSession`) terminates at the relay or gateway and therefore cannot protect data at rest. Whole-file AEAD fails because a consumer cannot verify the authentication tag until the entire multi-gigabyte bundle is downloaded. Conversely, naive per-chunk encryption without stream binding allows an attacker to reorder, truncate, or splice chunks from different generations.

Hoang, Reyhanitabar, Rogaway, and Vizár formalized the security of chunked streaming encryption in [Online Authenticated-Encryption and its Nonce-Reuse Misuse-Resistance (CRYPTO 2015, ePrint 2015/189)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/189), defining the `OAE2` security target and the `STREAM` construction deployed in [Google Tink Streaming AEAD](https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/third_party/tink/+/refs/heads/upstream/1.2/docs/PRIMITIVES.md) and [age file encryption](https://ocx.sh/catalog/age/age/). Subsequent random-access extensions (`nOAE2`) and Encrypt-then-Merkle designs such as [XChaCha20-Bao](https://github.com/identikey/recrypt/blob/main/docs/standards/xchacha20-bao-aead.md) highlight two critical architectural rules:

1. **Sequential `STREAM` (`age` / Tink) vs. Indexed Random-Access `nOAE2`**: Sequential `STREAM` increments a chunk counter with a 1-byte final-chunk flag (`0x01` on the last chunk), preventing truncation of full streams, but cannot authenticate whether a *randomly accessed* interior chunk actually belongs to the authorized version of the bundle unless the signed descriptor commits to the exact total length, chunk-group count, and Merkle root.
2. **Encrypt-then-Merkle (Ciphertext Tree + Signed Plaintext Manifest)**: If the outer SHA-384 Merkle tree is computed over the **encrypted chunk-group frames** (ciphertext + 16-byte [RFC 8439 ChaCha20-Poly1305](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8439/) tag), then untrusted custodial nodes, swarm peers, and HTTP caches can verify range proofs and detect corrupted uploads *without possessing the decryption key*, while the consumer verifies the Merkle inclusion proof *before* invoking `ChaCha20-Poly1305` decryption (`aead::decrypt_in_place`) and zeroes the buffer immediately upon any failure. Meanwhile, the encrypted inner descriptor (or restricted entry table) carries the plaintext SHA-384 digests of embedded entries so local `etalon-model-io` snapshots verify exact decoded bytes.

### 3.4 Prioritized Swarm/HTTP Transport, Modern Gateways, and Browser OPFS/WebGPU Constraints

External transport and browser standards establish concrete patterns for mapping native content-swarm operations onto HTTP and browser sandboxes:

- **Trustless Verifiable Gateways vs. Deserializing Proxies**: The [IPFS Trustless Gateway Specification](https://specs.ipfs.tech/http-gateways/trustless-gateway/) demonstrates that HTTP compatibility remains cryptographically sound only when gateways serve raw, byte-exact verifiable payloads (`application/vnd.ipld.raw` / `application/vnd.ipld.car` equivalents) rather than deserializing or re-encoding containers on the server. For `QBDL`, HTTP bindings must enforce `Accept-Encoding: identity`, exact [RFC 9110 §14](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9110/) byte ranges, and [RFC 9530 Digest Fields](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9530/) (`Repr-Digest` for the full immutable bundle representation vs. `Content-Digest` for the transferred partial range).
- **HTTP Extensible Prioritization and Media over QUIC**: [RFC 9218](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9218/) replaces broken HTTP/2 dependency trees with structured `Priority` parameters (`u=0..7` urgency and `i` incremental delivery). `QBDL`'s DAG priority tiers map deterministically onto RFC 9218 (`Tier 0` ontology/impostors/P64 headers $\to$ `u=0`; `Tier 1` mid-LOD/MoE router & shared experts $\to$ `u=2, i`; `Tier 2` hero `LOD0`/mip0/bulk weights $\to$ `u=5, i`). For live multi-consumer fan-out over QUIC ([RFC 9000](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9000/)), the IETF [`draft-ietf-moq-transport`](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-moq-transport/) (Media over QUIC, draft-23, November 2026) demonstrates named track/group/object publish-subscribe relays where relays forward encrypted objects by namespace/group priority without inspecting payloads—validating QDNF's separation of `qdnf:service:pubsub` generation announcements from immutable chunk-group payload delivery, while Cloudflare's QUIC ACK-validation research underscores why `QSession` ([RFC 9002](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9002/)-informed recovery) must enforce packet-number skipping and strict ACK range bounds against Optimistic ACK amplification.
- **Browser Dedicated Worker OPFS and WebGPU Limits**: Under the [WHATWG File System Standard](https://fs.spec.whatwg.org/) and [MDN `FileSystemSyncAccessHandle` specification](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FileSystemSyncAccessHandle), high-throughput synchronous random-access `read(buffer, { at })` and `write(buffer, { at })` into the Origin Private File System (OPFS) are restricted *exclusively to Dedicated Web Workers* inside secure contexts (`HTTPS` / `localhost`) and hold an exclusive file lock until `close()`. Similarly, [W3C WebGPU](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/) enforces strict adapter limit validation and exposes global VRAM pressure side-channels (§2.1.10). `Etalon`'s browser fixes—specifically `pop_error_scope` JS `null` safety and `Reflect::has` adapter limit sanitization (`etalon/README.md`), combined with `qualia-core-db`'s `OpfsCallbackRangeSource`—must anchor the browser streaming worker architecture so main UI threads never block and unsupported browser contexts fail cleanly before partial hydration begins.

### 3.5 Decentralized Quota Enforcement and Escrow Bounded Counters

Advisor Question A10 asks how multiple mirrors or gateways enforce a finite Permissive Commons compensation target $T$ ($S + W + H \le T$) without either exceeding $T$ (overshoot/overcharging) or requiring a synchronous global lock on every range request. Balegas et al. solved this exact invariant in [Extending Eventually Consistent Cloud Databases for Enforcing Numeric Invariants (IEEE SRDS 2015, arXiv:1503.09052)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.09052) using the **Bounded Counter CRDT**, building upon O'Neil's Escrow Transactional Method and recently refined for hierarchical Rust quota trees in [`bcounter` / `leasetree`](https://github.com/kostja/bcounter):

- **Zero-Overshoot Escrow Mechanics**: Instead of allowing replicas to debit a shared global counter optimistically (which permits up to $(N-1) \cdot R$ over-collection across $N$ mirrors during network partitions), the authoritative obligation steward partitions the remaining collectible cap $R = \max(0, T - S - W)$ into term-fenced, time-limited **escrow grants** $g_i$ assigned to active collector providers such that $\sum_{i=1}^N g_i \le R$ ($\Delta = 0$ strict zero-overshoot invariant). Each provider $i$ admits corporate recovery holds locally against its granted allowance $g_i$ with zero network round-trips on the packet path and reports settled usage upward via an idempotent grow-only per-node slot vector (taking $\max$ per slot on merge so no settled receipt is ever double-counted).
- **The $\sqrt{N}$ Safety-Stock Bound and Two-Regime Switching**: Inventory theory over $N$ decentralized nodes proves that avoiding per-request coordination requires aggregate safety slack proportional to $\sqrt{N}$ relative to a single coordinator; when $R$ shrinks near zero, stranded idle grants cause *false denials* (refusing a write at node $A$ while node $B$ holds unused grant). As documented in [`bcounter`'s threat and tail analysis](https://github.com/kostja/bcounter), hard billing caps must therefore operate in **two regimes**: (1) **Escrow Lease Regime** while $R$ comfortably exceeds $N \cdot q_{\text{chunk}}$, with automatic idle grant hand-back at $\mathrm{TTL}/2$ and leader-term fencing; and (2) **Tight-Path Authoritative Coordination** once $R \le \tau_{\text{tail}}$ (or when an uncached provider requests the final units), where reservations coordinate directly with the obligation steward—while personal humanitarian users remain completely unaffected (`ActingClass::PersonalHumanitarian` requires $A = 0$ recovery units and never blocks on cap reconciliation).

### 3.6 Canonical Mathematical Generalization and Quantum Optimization Caching

For reusable QPU/solver artifacts in the Permissive Commons, naive variable renaming fails both mathematical equivalence and privacy requirements. In combinatorial optimization ([QPLIB benchmark specification, Furini et al. / arXiv:2407.21140](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21140v1); [Quantum Optimization survey, arXiv:2312.02279](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02279v3)), a binary QUBO problem $\min_{x \in \{0,1\}^n} x^\top Q x$ maps bijectively to an Ising spin-glass Hamiltonian $H(\sigma) = \sum_i h_i \sigma_i + \sum_{i < j} J_{ij} \sigma_i \sigma_j + C$ over spins $\sigma_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ via $x_i = (\sigma_i + 1)/2$. Two superficially distinct QUBO matrices represent the *exact same physical optimization problem* under three gauge transformations:

1. **Positive Affine Energy Scaling**: Multiplying $(h, J)$ by $\alpha > 0$ and shifting $C$ preserves exact ground-state optima $\arg\min_\sigma H(\sigma)$ (though altering Boltzmann thermal sampling at inverse temperature $\beta$ unless $\beta$ scales by $1/\alpha$).
2. **Spin-Flip Gauge ($\mathbb{Z}_2^n$ Bit-Inversion)**: Replacing $x_i \mapsto 1 - x_i$ ($\sigma_i \mapsto -\sigma_i$) negates $h_i$ and incident couplings $J_{ij}$ while preserving the spectrum identically.
3. **Vertex Permutation ($S_n$ Graph Isomorphism)**: Relabeling variables via $\pi \in S_n$ permutes rows and columns of $(h, J)$.

McKay and Piperno's individualization-refinement algorithms in [Practical Graph Isomorphism, II (`nauty` and `Traces`, arXiv:1301.1493)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1493) ([Nauty and Traces software distribution](https://pallini.di.uniroma1.it/)) compute canonical representatives of vertex- and edge-colored graphs in milliseconds for hundreds to thousands of vertices—far exceeding `qualiadb`'s current `MAX_QUBO_VARS = 128` ceiling (`qubo_compiler.rs`). By quantizing normalized rational/dyadic couplings $(|h_i|, J_{ij})$ into deterministic color partitions and running bounded Weisfeiler-Leman color refinement followed by McKay–Piperno canonical labeling with a hard step budget, a node derives a deterministic SHA-384 **Canonical Problem Commitment** while keeping the secret witness $(\pi, s \in \{-1,+1\}^n, \alpha, C)$ strictly local for $O(n)$ solution rehydration.

## 4. Enumerated Best-Possible Solution Architecture (`qbdl-stream-pq-1`)

Synthesizing the codebase audit and external research yields one cohesive, concrete architecture—designated **`qbdl-stream-pq-1`**—that resolves the open questions in the initial brief without breaking existing `QBDL` v1 byte transparency.

### 4.1 Service Composition Boundary in QDNF

`QBDL` streaming must **extend `qdnf:service:content-swarm`** via negotiated capability profile `qdnf:feature:qbdl-stream-pq-1` (computed as a 48-byte SHA-384 `ServiceId` per `crates/qualia-core-db/src/net/peer/host/services.rs`) rather than allocating an independent transport protocol. Connection establishment, hybrid PQ key exchange, congestion control, path migration, and anti-amplification remain exclusively in `QSession` (`qsession-and-services.md`); path eligibility remains exclusively in `CSCP` (`draft-webcivics-cscp-00.md`); governed offer/consent negotiation remains in `Share` and `FetchHandle` (`session/fetch.rs`); generational update notifications remain in `qdnf:service:pubsub` and `QSync`.

To overcome the small-object bounds of `ContentManifest` (`MAX_RANGES = 8`, `MAX_DECODED_BYTES = 4 MiB` in `replication/manifest.rs`) without weakening hot-path zero-heap guarantees, QPR retains `ContentManifest` for control artifacts and introduces a cold-admitted, paged **`QbdlStreamPlan`** backed by fixed-size hot-path **`ChunkTransferTable`** slots (configurable per host profile from 8 slots on embedded/mobile WASM up to 256 slots on desktop/server NVMe peers, all charged against QPR's `ReservationLedger`).

### 4.2 Detached COSE + CBOR-LD Authenticated Stream Descriptor (QASD)

Every governed or PQ-conformant `QBDL` transfer begins by resolving and verifying a bounded, detached **Qualia Authenticated Stream Descriptor (QASD)** before any bulk payload or untrusted footer index bytes are parsed. A detached QASD preserves bit-for-bit immutability of existing `.hmc` files on disk, avoids circular self-hashing of embedded manifests, and allows recipient encryption envelopes or provider escrow leases to rotate without rewriting multi-gigabyte payload entries.

The QASD wire envelope standardizes QPR's open `CRY-02.05` proof structure using deterministic CBOR ([RFC 8949 §4.2.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8949/)) wrapped in a dual-signature COSE structure ([RFC 9052](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9052/)) carrying:

1. **Protected Signing Structure**: Two mandatory detached signatures over the identical deterministic-CBOR `QasdBody` and external associated data—one `ML-DSA-65` signature (`alg = -49` per [RFC 9964](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9964/), 3,309 bytes) and one `Ed25519` signature (`alg = -8`, 64 bytes)—enforcing `single_algorithm_is_enough() == false`. High-value root ontology or archival bundles may attach an optional third offline countersignature using `SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s` ([NIST FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final)).
2. **PQ Authority Root Binding (`did:qi` v2 Bridge)**: Until `did:qi` genesis identifiers migrate fully to SHA-384, a controller publishing under `qpr-pq-1` must present a `did:qi` document containing an authorized `DualProof` verification method (`ML-DSA-65` + `Ed25519`) anchored at creation or prior to any untrusted quantum exposure window, validated against a pinned trust anchor or invitation-scoped `did:qi` log (`did-qi-method.md`). Classical-only `did:qi` proofs are rejected for `qbdl-stream-pq-1` (`classical_only_authority_is_enough() == false`).
3. **Immutable Object and Layout Commitments**: Exact `bundle_byte_length` (`u64`), `qbdl_version` (`1`), required outer `alignment_log2` (`6` for 64B, `12` for 4 KiB, `14` for 16 KiB `.p64` zero-copy), SHA-384 of the 64-byte `QBDL` header, SHA-384 of the trailing CBOR index footer, SHA-384 of the complete bundle plaintext (via [RFC 9995](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9995/) hash-envelope semantics), and—when encrypted—SHA-384 of the encrypted bundle representation.
4. **Pinned Semantic & Governance Closure**: Exact SHA-384 digests of the [W3C CBOR-LD 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/cbor-ld-10/) compression dictionary, RDF/N3 ontology modules, [W3C SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/) shape graphs, aggregate `IngestAccessPolicy` (`PublicRedistributable`, `PublicNotForRedistribution`, `ProjectInternal`, or `Restricted`), and applicable `Obligation` / contract digest.
5. **Entry, Segment, Dependency DAG, and Etalon Execution Metadata**: Bounded inventory of entries, semantic segments, compatibility switch groups, topological prerequisite edges, and worst-case `etalon` memory/VRAM/decode budgets (`cpu_scratch_bytes`, `vram_upload_bytes`, `decompressed_ceiling_bytes`, `min_page_align_log2`).

### 4.3 Two-Tier Adaptive SHA-384 Range-Proof Construction

To resolve Advisor Question A04 optimally across both kilobyte digital-twin manifests and multi-gigabyte `.p64` weight bundles, `qbdl-stream-pq-1` standardizes **two deterministic range-proof modes** under a single `SHA-384` commitment discipline:

- **Mode 1 — Inline SHA-384 Segment Digest Array (`segment_count <= 32`)**: For compact bundles or few-entry packages (such as a standalone `.q42` lexical pack, solver witness, or small `.10d` prop), the QASD embeds up to 32 exact `(offset, length, sha384_digest)` tuples ($\le 1,536$ bytes of hashes). Verification requires zero Merkle proof bytes on payload transfers and a single streaming `SHA-384` pass per segment.
- **Mode 2 — Domain-Separated SHA-384 Chunk-Group Binary Merkle Tree (`segment_count > 32` or `bundle_byte_length > 2 MiB`)**: The bundle (plaintext if public, ciphertext frames if encrypted) is partitioned into fixed-size **chunk groups** of size $Q \in \{16\text{ KiB}, 64\text{ KiB}\}$ (`chunk_group_log2` = `14` when aligned to 16 KiB `.p64` pages and `TransferTable` blocks, or `16` = 64 KiB default for large `.emf`/`.m64`/`.10d` media). Each leaf $m \in [0, N-1]$ with exact byte length $\ell_m \in [1, Q]$ ($1 \le \ell_{N-1} \le Q$, $\ell_m = Q$ for $m < N-1$) is hashed with explicit context binding:
  $$L_m = \mathrm{SHA384}\bigl(\mathtt{0x00} \parallel \mathtt{"qbdl-pq1-leaf"} \parallel H_{\text{obj}} \parallel \mathrm{u64be}(m) \parallel \mathrm{u32be}(\ell_m) \parallel \mathrm{Bytes}_m\bigr)$$
  Internal nodes follow [RFC 9162 §2.1.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) exact binary subtrees:
  $$I(L, R) = \mathrm{SHA384}\bigl(\mathtt{0x01} \parallel L \parallel R\bigr)$$
  The QASD carries only the 48-byte `merkle_root`, `chunk_group_log2`, and `leaf_count` $N$. Providers supply compact [RFC 9162 §2.1.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) / [RFC 9942](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9942/) inclusion proofs alongside requested chunk-group ranges (or serve paged outboard subtrees cached in OPFS/disk). No byte is ever admitted to a parser, `WasmSliceQ42Reader`, `.10d` transcoder, or `etalon` buffer until its inclusion proof verifies against `merkle_root`.

### 4.4 Encrypt-then-Merkle Random-Access Segment AEAD (`qbdl-oae2-v1`)

To resolve Advisor Question A05 for private Sanctuary vaults, bilateral shares, and gated Commons custody over untrusted mirrors and HTTP gateways, `qbdl-stream-pq-1` specifies an **Encrypt-then-Merkle Random-Access Online Authenticated Encryption (`nOAE2`)** profile built strictly from QPR's existing primitives ([HKDF-SHA-384, RFC 5869](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc5869/) and [ChaCha20-Poly1305, RFC 8439](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8439/), informed by [Hoang et al. OAE2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/189)):

1. **Key Hierarchy and Recipient Envelopes**: A fresh 256-bit symmetric `DataEncryptionKey` ($\mathrm{DEK}$) is generated once per immutable ciphertext bundle. Per-chunk-group subkeys or base nonces derive via `HKDF-SHA-384` using domain label `"qbdl-pq1/oae2-dek"` and a random 32-byte `bundle_salt`. Recipient access grants live in detached **Recipient Key Envelopes** wrapped via QPR's hybrid `X25519 + ML-KEM-768` combiner ([NIST SP 800-227](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-227.pdf)) or pairwise `QSession` exporter keys. Adding or revoking a recipient updates detached envelopes and cache capability policy without re-encrypting the multi-gigabyte ciphertext object (re-encryption with a fresh $\mathrm{DEK}$ is required only when revoking prospective access to a future generation).
2. **Deterministic Chunk-Group Framing and Nonce Uniqueness**: Plaintext chunk group $m$ (up to $Q$ bytes) is encrypted under `ChaCha20-Poly1305` using a 96-bit nonce $\mathrm{Nonce}_m = \mathrm{NoncePrefix}_{32} \oplus (0^{24} \parallel \mathrm{u64be}(m) \parallel \mathrm{FinalByte}_m)$, where $\mathrm{FinalByte}_m = \mathtt{0x01}$ iff $m = N-1$ and $\mathtt{0x00}$ otherwise, and canonical Associated Authenticated Data ($\mathrm{AAD}_m$) binding `(protocol_id, ciphertext_bundle_id, generation, chunk_group_index = m, plaintext_len = \ell_m, is_final)`. Each encrypted chunk group expands by exactly 16 bytes (e.g., $65,536\text{ B} \to 65,552\text{ B}$), yielding a deterministic constant-stride byte offset mapping $m \mapsto m \cdot (Q + 16)$ for both native and HTTP `Range` requests!
3. **Dual Ciphertext/Plaintext Commitments**: The public/custodial QASD commits to the **ciphertext** SHA-384 Merkle root so untrusted relays, CDNs, and custody nodes verify stored and served slices without decryption keys. Inside the encrypted payload, chunk group 0 carries the **Confidential Entry Directory** committing the plaintext `QBDL` index, entry keys (preventing filename/organ/model-role metadata leakage to caches!), and per-entry plaintext SHA-384 digests. Decryption uses QPR's caller-buffered `aead::decrypt_in_place`; if either the outer Merkle proof or the Poly1305 tag fails, the destination `BufferLease` is zeroized before release.

### 4.5 Backward-Compatible QBDL Format Alignment and Unified Preflight

To preserve existing `QBDL` v1 files while supporting zero-copy `.p64` execution in `etalon`:

- **Extended Alignment Flags (`QBDL` Header Bytes `6..8`)**: Retain `0x0000` (64-byte alignment) and `0x0001` (`4096`-byte alignment), and allocate `0x0002` (`BUNDLE_FLAG_PAGE_ALIGN_16384`, 16,384-byte alignment matching `P64_DEFAULT_PAGE_SIZE`) plus `0x0004` (`BUNDLE_FLAG_EXTENDED_ALIGN`, reading explicit power-of-two `align_log2` in `6..=24` from reserved byte `40` when specialized `.p64` or GPU DMA pages require $> 16\text{ KiB}$ alignment). Update `BundleWriter` to auto-promote bundle alignment to $\max(\text{entry\_align})$ whenever a `.p64` entry is added.
- **Single Zero-Heap `minicbor` Index Preflight**: Replace `ciborium::from_reader` in `HmcProgressiveRangePlanner::from_header_and_index` with a shared `minicbor` preflight validator that accepts the defined alignment flags (`0x0000`, `0x0001`, `0x0002`, `0x0004`), enforces strictly increasing non-overlapping aligned entry intervals, rejects duplicate keys via bounded hash/sorted-index checks, enforces exact 32-byte legacy `sha256` fields, bounds `meta` depth/bytes, and rejects trailing bytes—without imposing the local 128 MiB `file_bytes` cap on remote `total_length: u64`.

### 5.2 Bounded Dependency DAG and Hardware-Coupled Adaptation

The existing three-tier enum (`StreamPriorityTier::{Tier0, Tier1, Tier2}`) in `streaming_loader.rs` is preserved as the coarse scheduling class, but upgraded into an explicit **Bounded Dependency DAG** inside the QASD (`max_dag_nodes = 256`, `max_edges = 1024`, topologically sorted by node ID so cycle detection runs in $O(V + E)$ linear pass with zero recursion). Every segment node declares:

1. **Hard Prerequisite Set**: Indices of segments whose verification and structural admission *must* complete before this segment is decoded (for example: `.m64` Bank 1 LUT before `Strata` chunks; `.10d` `SKL1` skeleton before skinned `LOD1` mesh; `.p64` header + `ETLIMD1\0` + `moe_router` + shared expert before routed `ExpertTile` planes).
2. **Equivalence vs. Approximation Relation**: Adaptation must never treat numerically or scientifically distinct objects as silent substitutes. The QASD types every alternative group as one of four strict relations:
   - `BitExactReencoding`: Lossless container/tile transform producing identical decoded bits (e.g., `lossless_tile::encode_tile_split_f16` vs. raw `F16`).
   - `PerceptualProxy`: Visual/auditory LOD (`SURF1` $\to$ `IMPO1` $\to$ `LOD2` $\to$ `LOD0`) permitted for viewport rendering but **forbidden** as input to metrological, clinical, or physics solvers.
   - `CalibratedQuantization`: Model or field precision tier (`Q4_K` vs. `Q8_0` vs. `BF16`) carrying an explicit `FidelityContract` error bound; switching mid-session is forbidden for stateful autoregressive KV caches (`etalon-inference::runtime::kv`) unless a declared clean checkpoint/re-prefill boundary is crossed.
   - `OntogeneticOrStateVariant`: Distinct biological or physical states in `TwinFamilyManifest` (`Male`/`Female`, `Juvenile`/`Adult`, `Pristine`/`Damaged`); **never** interchangeable under bandwidth adaptation.
3. **Coupled Etalon Admission & Backpressure**: Before `QSession` issues `RANGE_REQUEST` credits for a segment, the host reserves **both** QPR `ReservationLedger` network/verify bytes **and** the target consumer slot:
   - For GPU geometry/textures/compute: `etalon_resources::ResidencyGovernor::admit` reserves CPU staging and GPU VRAM bytes (evicting only unpinned LRU slots, never active in-flight frames) on a verified `SharedDeviceQueue` (`Device::owns_queue == Some(true)`).
   - For dense/MoE/PLE inference: `etalon_inference::runtime::budget` and `Qwen4ExpStoragePlan` reserve KV-cache and staging slabs; tentative steps run inside `dense_transaction::propose_dense` (`PendingDense`), committing only after Webizen policy approval, and emitting outputs solely through `GovernedArtifactSession` (`AcceptedArtifact`).
   - For local persistent models: once a `.p64` entry completes verification, `etalon_model_io::Snapshot` / `ModelLease` seals the file against concurrent OS writers and binds multi-part dependencies via `bundle_digest`.

## 6. Native QSession Lifecycle, Live Generations, and HTTP/Browser Compatibility Binding

### 6.1 Native Exchange Lifecycle and Zero-Double-Recovery Discipline

Over native QDNF, `qbdl-stream-pq-1` maps cleanly onto `QSession` multiplexed reliable streams (`qsession-and-services.md`) and `Share` governance:

1. **`STREAM_DESCRIBE` / `SHARE_OFFER`**: Initiator resolves target resource/provider via `QResolve`/`QSR`, applies `CSCP` **exclude-then-rank** path filtering (eliminating prohibited jurisdictions, unencrypted relays, or non-consented locators *before* RTT/bandwidth ranking), completes the 6-stage `qpr-pq-1` hybrid handshake (`pq_handshake.rs`), and requests the QASD bound to `(service_id, resource_iri, policy_epoch, acting_class)`.
2. **Cold Descriptor Verification & Closure Compilation**: Cold worker verifies the dual `COSE_Sign1` proof (`ML-DSA-65` + `Ed25519`), checks `did:qi` controller authority, verifies pinned CBOR-LD ontology/SHACL digests from local cache (triggering bounded cold dependency fetches if absent), evaluates aggregate `IngestAccessPolicy`, and compiles a deterministic `QbdlStreamPlan` plus economic `ReservationHandle`.
3. **`STREAM_OPEN` & Credit-Paced `SEGMENT_FETCH`**: Initiator opens one control stream and bounded data streams prioritized by `(tier, topological_depth, utility_per_byte)`. Each request specifies `(object_digest, generation, chunk_group_start, chunk_group_count, need_inclusion_proof, cancel_epoch, receipt_op_id)`.
4. **Proof-First Arrival Pipeline**: Arriving chunk groups land in pre-reserved `BufferLease` slots. The verifier checks exact byte bounds, verifies the SHA-384 Merkle inclusion proof (or Mode 1 array entry), runs `ChaCha20-Poly1305` `decrypt_in_place` when encrypted, and transitions the slot to `PlaintextReleased`—zeroizing immediately on any mismatch.
5. **Transport Recovery Separation**: `QSession` owns packet loss detection and congestion control ([RFC 9002](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9002/) profile with skipped packet numbers against Optimistic ACK). The QBDL layer **never** runs a competing retransmission timer over an open reliable stream; it intervenes only on stream reset, provider stall/migration, or explicit `STREAM_CANCEL` (bumping `cancel_epoch` so late in-flight frames drop cleanly without resurrecting cancelled work or double-charging creation recovery).

### 6.2 Live Generation Publication vs. Unfinalized Producer Streaming

Consistent with Advisor Question A12, `qbdl-stream-pq-1` separates three delivery modes and designates the first two for initial conformance:

- **Mode I — Immutable Progressive Retrieval (Mandatory Baseline)**: Partial, dependency-ordered retrieval of a finalized immutable `QBDL` bundle.
- **Mode II — Live Generational Cuts over `pubsub` + `QSync` (Recommended Live Mechanism)**: Producers (such as `qualia-etalon-sensing-bridge` publishing periodic RF/acoustic/telemetry containers or dynamic digital-twin updates) emit successive immutable micro-bundles or delta generations $G_0, G_1, G_2, \dots$. Each QASD carries `sequence_no` (strictly monotonic `u64`), `predecessor_qasd_digest`, `availability_window_ms`, `keyframe_cut: bool`, and an optional [RFC 9162 §2.1.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) Merkle consistency proof when extending a growing bundle collection. Subscribers follow `qdnf:service:pubsub` (`semantic-peer-services.md` §2.1); if retention expires between $G_k$ and $G_{k+m}$, the service emits an explicit `GapDiscontinuity { missed_range: (k+1)..m, resnapshot_generation }` event—never silently stitching incompatible state.
- **Mode III — Unfinalized Append-Streaming (`QBDLS` Extension, Deferred Separate Profile)**: Because `QBDL` v1 places its `index_offset`, `total_length`, and `crc32c` in bytes `0..64` at file start, an unfinished `QBDL` v1 file cannot be streamed before finalization without rewriting byte 0. Rather than mutating `QBDL` v1 or pretending it supports live byte-append, low-latency sub-second live tracks should either emit MoQ-inspired ([`draft-ietf-moq-transport`](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-moq-transport/)) independent chunk-group objects per generation under Mode II, or negotiate a separate future `QBDL` v2 streaming profile (`magic = "QBD2"`) using checkpointed append-only Merkle tree heads.

### 6.3 Backward-Compatible HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2, HTTP/3, and Browser Binding

When a native peer accesses an HTTP mirror via the Legacy Internet Gateway (`legacy-internet-gateway.md`) or runs inside a browser [W3C WebGPU](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/) portal, the HTTP binding translates admitted `QbdlStreamPlan` operations without creating a weaker authority path:

1. **Pre-Pinned QASD Bootstrap**: An HTTP `ETag`, `Last-Modified`, or Web PKI TLS certificate is **never** accepted as `QBDL` publisher authority. The client obtains the dual-signed QASD first (from a native peer, a pinned `did:qi`/Q42 reference, or `<uri>.qasd.cose` verified against a trusted PQ controller key) and pins `bundle_byte_length` and `merkle_root` before issuing any HTTP `Range` request.
2. **Strict Byte-Exact Range Framing ([RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9110/) & [RFC 9530](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9530/))**:
   - Every range request uses single-interval inclusive syntax `Range: bytes=<start>-<end>` where `end = start.checked_add(len)?.checked_sub(1)?` and `len > 0`, paired with `Accept-Encoding: identity` (forbidding transparent `gzip`/`br`/`zstd` middlebox re-encoding that shifts offsets) and `If-Range: "<strong-etag>"` when available. Multipart `multipart/byteranges` responses are rejected by default to eliminate MIME boundary-parsing DoS vectors.
   - Servers and LIG caches should emit [RFC 9530](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9530/) `Repr-Digest: sha-384=:<base64>:` (matching the QASD representation commitment) and `Content-Digest: sha-384=:<base64>:` for the partial slice, while clients still verify the QASD's SHA-384 Merkle inclusion proof end-to-end (treating untrusted HTTP servers identically to [IPFS Trustless Gateways](https://specs.ipfs.tech/http-gateways/trustless-gateway/)).
   - Status handling is fail-closed: `206 Partial Content` must match the exact `Content-Range: bytes <start>-<end>/<bundle_byte_length>` header and exact byte count; an ignored-range `200 OK` is **aborted immediately** before reading the body unless `bundle_byte_length` fits within an explicit small-object fallback budget ($\le 1\text{ MiB}$); `416 Range Not Satisfiable` or validator mismatch triggers provider failover within `CSCP` scope.
3. **Priority, Redirects, and Cache Partitioning**:
   - Emit [RFC 9218](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9218/) `Priority` headers (`u=0` for `Tier 0`, `u=2, i` for `Tier 1`, `u=5, i` for `Tier 2`) so HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 ([RFC 9114](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9114/)) multiplexers schedule urgent ontology/header chunks ahead of bulk weights.
   - Every HTTP `3xx` redirect must pass `FetchHandle` (`session/fetch.rs`) and `CSCP` path re-evaluation before following; authorization headers, capability tokens, and recipient identifiers are **never** forwarded across origin changes.
   - Shared and LIG caches ([RFC 9111](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9111/)) key entries by `(ciphertext_or_plaintext_root, profile_id, privacy_partition_id)`—never serving a cached private slice across distinct personas merely because the underlying content hash matches (preventing cross-user existence/deduplication side-channels).
4. **Browser Dedicated Worker, OPFS, and Etalon WebGPU Integration**:
   - Browser hydration runs inside a **Dedicated Web Worker** owning a synchronous [`FileSystemSyncAccessHandle`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FileSystemSyncAccessHandle) in the [WHATWG Origin Private File System (OPFS)](https://fs.spec.whatwg.org/), feeding verified slices into `OpfsCallbackRangeSource` (`q42/volume/opfs_source.rs`) without copying full bundles onto the WASM linear heap.
   - Cross-origin isolation (`Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp`) is probed upfront when multi-worker `SharedArrayBuffer` hydration is requested; when absent, the runtime falls back deterministically to single-worker message-transferred `ArrayBuffer` leases rather than panicking.
   - `Etalon` browser WebGPU initialization enforces `Reflect::has` limit sanitization and null-safe `pop_error_scope` (`etalon/README.md`), feeding streamed weight chunks through `browser_prefill` / `browser_projection` async GEMM pipelines within explicit adapter `maxBufferSize` and `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` ceilings.
   - Optional [HLS (RFC 8216)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8216/) and [DASH-IF](https://dashif.org/Guidelines-TimingModel/) playlists are generated strictly by a local Loopback/LIG presentation adapter for `HCF` audiovisual entries; stock media players never parse raw `.q42`, `.p64`, or `.emf` entries directly.

## 7. Permissive Commons Governance, Ontological Contracts, and Escrow-Bounded Economics

### 7.1 Aggregate Bundle Disclosure and Dual-Stage Ecosystem Licensing

`QBDL` bundles frequently package multiple entries—such as a public species morphology `.10d`, an Indigenous Traditional Knowledge / Biocultural (`tk_bc_labels.rs`) oral-history audio sidecar, a clinical or Sanctuary `.q42` graph, or an `etalon` compute shader (`ETALON-PLAN-03`). $$\mathrm{Policy}(\text{Bundle}) = \bigwedge_{e \in \text{Entries} \cup \{\text{Index}, \text{QASD}, \text{Sidecars}\}} \mathrm{Policy}(e)$$

- **Zero Partial-Row Masking on Shared Byte Objects**: A bundle may be marked `PublicRedistributable` (`FLAG_PERMISSIVE_COMMONS`) if and only if *every* embedded entry, index `key` string, `meta` blob, thumbnail, provenance record, and QASD field is explicitly approved as `PublicRedistributable`. Omitting a restricted entry from a detached descriptor while serving the underlying unencrypted `.hmc` byte object via HTTP `Range` or swarm blocks is strictly forbidden because byte-gap offsets trivially leak the hidden entry. - **Reconciling Interim and Target Ecosystem Licences**: As documented in `etalon/dev-docs/03-licensing-and-rights-architecture.md` (`ETALON-PLAN-03`), Etalon-specific code currently operates under an interim `CC BY-NC-ND 4.0` baseline pending full qualification of the MediaProphet Permissive Commons rights fabric. ### 7.2 Pinned CBOR-LD Ontological Contracts

Contractual terms governing a stream must conform to `ontological-contracts.md` encoded in [W3C CBOR-LD 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/cbor-ld-10/), mapping permissions, prohibitions, and duties onto [W3C ODRL 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/) and validating graph structure against [W3C SHACL Core](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/):

- **Replacing the Hardcoded `CommonsObligation` Helper Cap**: The legacy helper `CommonsObligation::is_discharged` (`cbor_ld_manifest.rs`), which passes a hardcoded `20` percent cap to `socioeconomics::commons_cost`, is deprecated as an authority mechanism. Authoritative targets $T$, accepted compensation/return ceilings, and operation classifications (`Exempt`, `SponsorFunded`, `ContributionRequired`, `Fulfilled`, `Unresolved` per `finite-project-compensation.md`) are compiled from the signed CBOR-LD contract into an immutable `AgreementHandle` before streaming starts.

### 7.3 Two-Regime Escrow Bounded-Counter Protocol for Finite Compensation

To enforce the finite project-recovery invariant $S + W + H \le T$ across $N$ concurrent decentralized mirrors/providers without per-packet settlement calls or corporate over-collection (`Advisor Question A10`), `qbdl-stream-pq-1` extends `Obligation` (`net/qdnf/economics/obligation.rs`) with a **Two-Regime Term-Fenced Escrow Protocol** grounded in [Balegas et al. Bounded Counters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.09052) and [`bcounter` hierarchical lease trees](https://github.com/kostja/bcounter):

| Regime & condition | Protocol mechanics | Guarantees for corporate & humanitarian actors |
|---|---|---|
| **Humanitarian / Personal Fast Path** (`ActingClass` $\in$ `{Personal, ProtectedPerson, VerifiedHumanitarian}`) | Verified via minimal-disclosure eligibility credential (`finite-project-compensation.md` §3). Recovery component $A = 0$ unconditionally; operating cost $O$ met locally, reciprocally, or via community sponsor pool. | **Never blocks on obligation state, mirror partitions, or cap exhaustion**; zero behavioral tracking or wallet requirement. |
| **Regime 1 — Decentralized Escrow Grants** ($R = T - S - W > \tau_{\text{tail}}$, where $\tau_{\text{tail}} = \max(4 \cdot q_{\text{unit}}, \lceil c \sqrt{N} \cdot q_{\text{unit}} \rceil)$) | The authoritative Obligation Steward issues dual-signed, term-fenced (`epoch_term`, `ttl_ms`) **Escrow Lease Grants** $g_i$ to authorized mirrors such that $\sum_i g_i \le R$. Mirror $i$ reserves corporate holds $h_{i,k}$ locally in $O(1)$ against $g_i$, renews at $\mathrm{TTL}/2$, hands back idle surplus $> 2 q_{\text{unit}}$, and merges settled receipts upward in an idempotent per-mirror monotonic CRDT slot vector. | **Strict zero overshoot** ($\sum S + W + H \le T$ holds even under arbitrary network partitions); zero network coordination on segment transfers; duplicate retransmissions deduplicated by `receipt_op_id = SHA384(agreement \|\| obligation \|\| range_set \|\| session_nonce)`. |
| **Regime 2 — Tight-Path Tail Coordination** ($0 < R \le \tau_{\text{tail}}$ or mirror holds $g_i = 0$) | Mirror pauses autonomous escrow draws and requests an exact atomic hold `reserve_hold(amount = min(quote, R - H))` from the Steward (expanding `Obligation::MAX_OPS` from 16 fixed slots to a bounded ring/journal backed by Q42 persistence). Once $S + W = T$, the Steward publishes a permanent dual-signed **`FulfilmentCertificate`** via `QSync`, setting $A = 0$ globally forever. | Eliminates the $\sqrt{N}$ false-denial tail near cap completion; guarantees exact final-cent discharge (`concurrent_last_payments_conserve_cap`) and permanent transition to zero creation-recovery fees. |
| **Partitioned / Offline Mirror Fallback** (Steward unreachable & local $g_i$ expired) | Stale mirrors **must never invent recovery holds or collect unescrowed creation fees**. Policy in the QASD selects explicitly between `ServeOperatingOnlyDeferRecovery` (for essential/educational Commons) or `HoldAdmissionUntilLeaseRenewal` (for commercial corporate gated tiers). | Prevents split-brain over-collection and ensures network outages never fabricate unagreed debt. |

Fractional carriage and recovery rates per MiB use checked 64-bit integer micro-units with deterministic carry-accumulator rounding per `(agreement_id, object_id)` session so splitting a 64 MiB download into 1,024 small range requests produces the *exact same* cumulative charge as one contiguous stream.

## 8. Canonical Mathematical Generalization and the QPU Commons Pipeline

To align `qubo_compiler.rs`, `qpu_pipeline.rs`, and `qpu_dispatcher.rs` with Webizen Permissive Commons §2.4–2.5 (`Advisor Question A11`), `qbdl-stream-pq-1` replaces direct-to-vendor QPU submission with a rigorous five-stage **Local-First, Commons-Second, QPU-Last** pipeline.

### 8.1 Two-Tier Canonical Ising/QUBO Generalization and Local Witness Retention

When a local problem passes the classical solver cascade (`solvers/optimization`, `solvers/quantum_optimizers`, `solve_classical`) with an unresolved residual warranting escalation:

1. **Exact Dyadic Normalization & Ising Conversion**: Reject `NaN`/`Inf` entries in `QuboMatrix` (`qubo_compiler.rs`) and convert `Q` into symmetric Classical Ising Spin-Glass form $H(\sigma) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i \sigma_i + \sum_{0 \le i < j < n} J_{ij} \sigma_i \sigma_j + C$ ([QPLIB Ising mapping](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21140v1)). Normalize $(h, J)$ by $M = \max(\max_i |h_i|, \max_{i<j} |J_{ij}|) > 0$ into signed 64-bit fixed-point integers $q_i, K_{ij} \in [-2^{52}, 2^{52}]$ at explicit precision tolerance $\varepsilon_{\text{quant}}$.
2. **Spin-Flip Sign Canonicalization & McKay–Piperno Graph Labeling**: Construct an edge- and vertex-colored undirected graph $G = (V, E, c_V, c_E)$ encoding $|q_i|$, $\mathrm{sgn}(q_i)$ gauge invariants, and $K_{ij}$, and execute bounded 1-WL color refinement plus [McKay–Piperno individualization-refinement (`nauty`/`Traces` algorithm)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1493) under a hard deterministic fuel ceiling (`max_ir_nodes = 16,384`).
   - **Canonical Problem Key**: $K_{\text{prob}} = \mathrm{SHA384}(\mathtt{"qbdl-qpu-canon-v1"} \parallel \text{problem\_class} \parallel \mathrm{CBOR}(G_{\text{canon}}, \varepsilon_{\text{quant}}))$.
   - **Private Rehydration Witness (Never Transmitted)**: The local vault retains $W_{\text{priv}} = (\pi \in S_n, s \in \{-1,+1\}^n, M, C, \text{index\_map}: \text{Var} \leftrightarrow \text{NQuin})$, wiping `index_map` from solver egress memory before any network lookup occurs.
3. **Structural Disclosure Assessment**: Even stripped of IRI names, numerical coupling topology (such as a proprietary supply-chain or clinical contact graph) can leak sensitive structure. Before querying external Commons catalogs, QPolicy checks `DisclosureAssessment(G_canon)` (requiring $k$-core / entropy thresholds or explicit user/contract disclosure consent).

### 8.2 Commons Lookup, Solution Verification Taxonomy, and QBDL Result Packaging

Scoped lookup searches local Q42 caches first, then queries authorized QDNF Permissive Commons indexes (`QResolve`/`QSR`) using a privacy-preserving prefix bucket (or relay-blinded query) for $K_{\text{prob}}$:

- **Distinguished Lookup Outcomes**: `ExactVerifiedHit`, `ParameterizedFamilyCandidate` (requiring substitution witness check), `InFlightReservationJoin` (deduplicating concurrent identical jobs via QPR lease), `InsufficientQualityHit` (e.g., heuristic sample found when certified optimum was required), `ScopedMiss` (not found in searched catalogs—never claimed as proof of mathematical novelty), and `LookupUnavailable`.
- **Mandatory Local Mathematical Verification (Zero Trust in Quantum Provenance)**: When a candidate `QBDL` solver bundle is streamed from the Commons, the consumer verifies QASD PQ signatures and SHA-384 range proofs, then **independently validates the mathematical solution** against its private $H(\sigma)$ using checked exact/interval arithmetic (`qubo_compiler::calculate_energy`):

| Solution assurance class | Required QBDL bundle entries & verification gate | Permitted downstream consumption |
|---|---|---|
| **`CertifiedOptimum`** | `problem/canonical.cbor`, `solution/assignment.bin`, `certificate/dual_bound_proof.cbor` (branch-and-bound / SDP dual witness verifying primal-dual gap $\le \varepsilon$) | Admissible for safety-critical, fiduciary, and exact scientific derivations. |
| **`BoundedApproximation`** | Assignment + verified dual lower bound $L$ proving $(E(\sigma^*) - L)/|L| \le \rho$, plus solver/compiler provenance | Admissible when query tolerance accepts approximation ratio $\rho$. |
| **`FeasibleHeuristic`** | Assignment passing all hard constraint checks ($E_{\text{penalty}} = 0$) and beating local classical baseline (`solve_classical`), with hardware shot/anneal telemetry | Admissible for routing, scheduling, and heuristic optimization (`QuantumTaskKind::QuboRouting`). |
| **`SampledDistribution`** | Empirical bitstring histogram (`samples.bin`), shot count, calibration artifact digest, and effective inverse temperature $\beta$ (rejecting uncalibrated positive objective rescaling!) | Admissible for Boltzmann/variational sampling (`Vqe`, `DftGroundState`) within declared statistical error bars. |

Only upon a verified `ScopedMiss` or `InsufficientQualityHit`—and after acquiring an explicit `ReservationHandle` covering provider cost, shot budget, and disclosure—does `qpu_dispatcher` submit the canonicalized blind problem to an external QPU. Validated new solutions eligible for Commons contribution are packaged as immutable `QBDL` bundles carrying a finite `Obligation` limited strictly to actual audited QPU shot/compute cost (preventing perpetual rent on mathematical truths while fairly recovering the contributor's scarce quantum expenditure).

## 9. Runtime Budgets, Sparse Storage, Privacy Side-Channels, and Evidence Retention

### 9.1 Unifying QPR Cold/Hot Tiers with Etalon Memory Ownership

All streaming code paths must obey the strict two-tier execution discipline across `qualiadb` (`peer-runtime-api.md`) and `etalon` (`prepared-compute.md`, `inference-storage.md`, `etalon-resources`):

- **Cold Bounded Preparation**: QASD CBOR/COSE decoding, `ML-DSA-65` + `Ed25519` verification, CBOR-LD/SHACL compilation, `QbdlStreamPlan` DAG topological sort, `Snapshot`/`OPFS` sparse file creation, and `Adapter::prepare_compute` WGSL compilation execute on cold worker pools under explicit caller-owned byte, instruction-fuel, and wall-clock budgets. A flood of distinct descriptors or uncached ontology IRIs hits `ChunkTable` (`MAX_GLOBAL_PENDING = 32`, `MAX_PER_LOCATOR = 2` in `crypto/network/chunks.rs`) and returns bounded backoff without allocating on the packet thread.
- **Zero-Allocation Hot State Transitions**: Once `QbdlStreamPlan` is admitted, hot kernel transitions—frame header parsing, generation checks, `ChunkTransferTable` slot matching, SHA-384 incremental/leaf hashing, `ChaCha20-Poly1305` in-place decryption, `ResidencyGovernor::touch`, and `PreparedCompute::execute`—perform **zero Rust heap allocations** on success, rejection, cancellation, or timeout paths.
- **64-Bit Streaming Ceilings vs. 32-Bit WASM Addressability**: Negotiated descriptor limits cleanly separate `max_bundle_bytes: u64` (up to $1\text{ TiB}$ on 64-bit native NVMe hosts) from `max_resident_window_bytes: usize` (bounded below $2\text{ GiB}$ on `wasm32-unknown-unknown` per `etalon-model/src/operator_package/segment.rs` platform-fit checks and browser WebGPU buffer caps).

### 9.2 Crash-Safe Sparse Storage and Verified Checkpoints

Partial downloads must never confuse unwritten sparse filesystem holes with verified all-zero payload regions (`0x00...00`). Local native and OPFS stores maintain an append-only or double-buffered **Verified Chunk-Group Bitmap** alongside the sparse data file, sealed by a local HMAC/SHA-384 checkpoint binding `(qasd_digest, ciphertext_or_plaintext_root, chunk_group_log2, verified_bitmap, policy_epoch, storage_generation)`. On crash recovery or session resume:

1. Revalidate current `QPolicy` and `AgreementHandle` authority (an unchanged URL or cached QASD never bypasses revoked consent or expired leases).
2. Re-verify persisted chunks if backing exclusion was not continuously held (using `etalon_model_io::ModelLease` on supported local filesystems to guarantee zero external writer mutation while open, and re-hashing dirty trailing chunks after unclean shutdowns).
3. Expose an entry or bundle to downstream consumers via atomic rename/handle promotion only when its required dependency closure bits in `verified_bitmap` are all `1`. Garbage collection respects active `ModelLease` pins, custodial storage commitments (`container_custody`), and legal/clinical evidence holds (`net/qdnf/evidence/holds.rs`).

### 9.3 Traffic-Analysis Mitigations and Scoped Evidence Retention

Even under `qpr-pq-1` hybrid encryption and `qbdl-oae2-v1`, byte sizes, timing, and selective range-offset patterns can leak which organ in an anatomy pack, which geographic Hilbert tile in an `.m64` map, or which specialist LoRA/MoE adapter in an `operator_package` a user is inspecting:

- **Quantized Chunk-Group Reads & Optional Dyadic Padding**: Aligning all wire requests to 16 KiB or 64 KiB chunk-group multiples hides fine-grained sub-entry struct offsets (`NQuin` row or `.10d` section headers). For `Sanctuary`, `Clinical` (`net/qdnf/clinical/`), and `ProtectedPerson` profiles, QPolicy enables optional **dyadic range-cover padding** (rounding small fetches to power-of-two chunk-group blocks or fetching decoy sibling groups within a bounded cover budget `profiles/cover.rs`) paired with `nym_adapter.rs` / `CSCP` privacy-preserving relays—without claiming perfect mathematical anonymity or zero-knowledge oblivious RAM where none exists.
- **Evidence vs. Telemetry Hygiene (`ADR 0011` & [RFC 9942](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9942/))**: Operational metrics (time-to-Tier-0 readiness, wasted cancelled bytes, SHA-384/AEAD verification microseconds, `ResidencyGovernor` evictions, cap-escrow false denials) aggregate locally without per-user behavioral logs. Retained operational receipts (`COSE` receipts per [RFC 9942](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9942/) and `net/qdnf/evidence/seal.rs`) record pinned QASD/contract digests, policy epoch, and final completion/gap outcomes while **never persisting ephemeral session traffic secrets, private `DataEncryptionKey`s, or QPU rehydration witnesses**.
